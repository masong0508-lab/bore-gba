# BORE: GODGUY (Nintendo DS)

A god game spun off from BORE: shape a 128 x 128 world with the stylus, drop in life, and watch it live, eat, hunt and breed.

**GLOBE (v0.9).** The 3D globe is now drawn by the CPU, not the 3D hardware, so it works in every build (including the DraStic one): a lit planet with a dark side and city lights, atmosphere, drifting clouds that follow the weather, a sparkle on the water and stars; touch picking and walk mode use the same maths backwards (tested: walking faces every tile to within 1 tile). The cube world and ring world are gone; the pause menu was redrawn with a dimmed backdrop and the page counter replaced the dots.

**DS BUILDS (which one to use).** `godguy_hw.nds` (made by `build_hw.sh`) is the one that runs in DraStic: it uses NO libnds and NO calico (own ARM9 start-up, own ARM7 for sound and touch, direct hardware registers, the top screen as a tile layer) in the classic ROM layout. It has no SD saving (use DraStic's save states). `godguy.nds` (made by `build_rom.sh` / `make`) is the libnds 2 / calico build for emulators and hardware that support it (melonDS, recent flashcarts) and has saving and the globe. `godguy_hw_safe.nds` is the hardware build with all sound off, for finding crashes.

**v0.8: A CAST, WEATHER, WALKING THE GLOBE, BETTER SPRITES, A BETTER FLOW.**
- **Narrative**: a cast with portraits and typed dialogue (Wisp, Mira, Gruul, Elowen, Thorgrim, the Hollow King): a scene at the start of every chapter and an epilogue before the ending and credits. The Hollow King is the last god before you, who gave up; the story is about what to do with him.
- **Sprites**: 22 new creature shapes (each of the eight peoples now looks different; wolf, boar, sheep, cow; imp, werewolf, cyclops, demon, lich, hydra, scorpion, troll), crowns on elite and boss monsters.
- **Weather**: rain, storms (lightning strikes the land and flashes the screen), snow; it dims the light, greens dirt, kills fire and has its own music. Rain and snow are sprites.
- **Walking the globe** (3D): Y + A turns walking on; the pad moves an avatar tile by tile, the planet turns to keep it in front of the camera, A uses the current power on it. Checked on the PC (sphere: every tile faced within one tile); the cube world is not accurate enough so walking uses the sphere.
- **Flow**: START opens a pause menu (save, volume, difficulty, monsters, mode, new world, help, title) with confirmations, so a new world is never one stray button away; tool descriptions; a picker panel showing the choice before and after for spawn / gift / mutate; page dots; toast messages.

**v0.7: SUBSPECIES, A REAL INTERFACE, THE 3DS.**
- **23 heritable subspecies traits** (venomous, thick-hided, swift, giant, tiny, fire-born, amphibious, winged, pack, regenerating, camouflaged, spitting, symbiotic...). Children inherit their parent's traits and now and then mutate; a creature can carry two. The MUTATE power (X / Y picks a trait you have met) makes a subspecies on purpose; page 5 of the top screen is the codex with live counts. These are my own design: the WorldBox wiki page could not be read from here (it sits behind a bot check), so nothing is copied from it. Monsters now also come as ELITE and BOSS ranks.
- **Interface**: the text console is gone. The top screen is a drawn interface (title screen with a spinning planet, status panels with the minimap, bars and icons, story / bestiary / codex / sculptures / debug / help windows with scroll bars), and the touch screen has a **toolbar with 10 power icons** you tap (arrows scroll; the 12 ground-painting powers show the ground they paint). The DS cannot write single bytes to VRAM, so every bitmap, tile and sprite is built in RAM and copied 16 bits at a time (the earlier build did not do this and would have drawn garbage on hardware).
- **Intro, ending and credits scenes**, a one-line narration for each chapter, tips for new players, a help page, difficulty (calm / normal / brutal), world seeds in the debug menu.
- **3DS port**: `3ds/build.sh` builds `godguy.3dsx` with devkitARM + libctru. The game runs unchanged on top of `compat/ndsshim.h`, a small stand-in for the parts of libnds it uses (tile layers, sprites, the 16 sound channels, keys, stylus). The same shim lets the real DS program run on a PC (`pc_ds.c`) with scripted input, screenshots and the sound it makes. The 3D globe is DS-only. PSP and Vita are not done: no SDK for them here.
- Tests: `tools/run_tests.sh` now also runs the real program headless.

**v0.6: THE REMIXES.** Every one of the 26 original BORE tracks gets two new versions with new, slightly familiar names (SUNMAN SUNRISE -> SUNWOMAN SUNSET and SUNMAN'S SECOND DAWN, THE TICKING BOMB -> THE TICKING CLOCK and THE DEFUSED BOMB, MISTER QUICKBULLET -> MISTER SLOWBULLET and MISS QUICKBULLET...): a REMIX (re-keyed up a fourth, new tempo, groove, bass and sounds, built from the song's other hooks) and a REBIRTH (the opposite mode, a quieter or stranger form with a neighbouring song's hooks woven in; one in three is a long minimalist piece). 52 tracks, 134 in all. They stay locked in the Aural Sculptures until the game is 100%: the story finished, all 320 monsters met and all 320 slain. Then they also join the in-game music. The debug menu can mark everything slain or set 100% directly.

**v0.5.1: AURAL SCULPTURES AND DEBUG.** The jukebox is now the *Aural Sculptures* menu (page 3 of the top screen): it unlocks by finishing story chapter 3 (or with the debug code), plays any of the 82 tracks and also tests any of the 29 sound effects (X / Y). The code UP UP DOWN DOWN LEFT LEFT RIGHT B A START on the title screen unlocks the **debug menu** (page 4): sim speed x1/2/4/8, freeze, set the year, chapter and item tier, unlock all items, monsters on/off, spawn any of the 320 monsters at the camera, kill or meet them all, heal and feed everyone, fill village stores, found a village, add stats, finish the chapter now, save / load / erase the save (B+A), play any effect, lock or open the sculptures, and a live creature / tick / seed readout. Both flags are kept in the save file (format version 2).

**v0.5: ENEMIES, STORY, SAVES, BIG PLANETS, 82 TRACKS.**
- **320 monsters** (20 kinds x 16 colours, from imps and ghouls to krakens, liches and the elder dragon; four new sprite shapes). They rise from rifts far from the villages, hunt people first, burn houses and are shot by towers; stronger kinds come as the world ages and as you finish chapters. Villagers fight back, and gifts of weapons and armour decide who wins.
- **Story**: 14 chapters (use your powers, found a people, build homes, meet and slay monsters, give gifts, grow a hall, reach years...). Each chapter you finish opens a tier of items for good, so free play only has what the story has earned (L+R+START opens everything). Page 1 of the top screen (SELECT) shows the chapter; page 2 is the **bestiary** (320 entries, ? until met, * when slain).
- **Saving**: the whole world, villages, bestiary and story go to `godguy.sav` on the SD card (libfat) every year; L+R+SELECT saves now; the title screen continues it. Tested on the PC: a loaded game runs on exactly like the unsaved one (all three planet sizes), and damaged or truncated files are refused.
- **Planets of 128, 192 and 256 tiles a side** (Y on the title screen); the 3D globe, minimap and picking scale to any size.
- **Music: 82 tracks and 29 sound effects**, amalgamated from the visible songs of BORE (tools/gm_analyze.py reads the .xm files for each song's tempo, key, mode and repeated hooks; tools/gm2_compose.py re-keys, re-modes and re-times them). 38 tracks are for places and moments (title, each biome, time of day, wars, each boss, chapter jingles, credits), 32 are full songs of 3-6 minutes (intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, breakdown, second verse, key-lift chorus, a bridge in the opposite mode, final chorus, outro) and 12 are long minimalist pieces of 3-10 minutes (cycling arpeggios, additive process, phasing, 3 against 4). The engine plays 10 looped wave voices (lead, ping-pong echo, counter, harmony, two detuned pads, bass, arpeggio, sparkle, and a Haas double of the lead), a synthesised sampled drum kit on four slots and the effects on a PSG pulse and the noise channel, in stereo: tested on the PC for clipping (none), left/right balance (mean 0.06) and width. Page 3 of the top screen is a **jukebox**.
- Keys: SELECT page, X+Y toggles the 3D globe, SELECT+START toggles story / free play (new world), START new world.
- Tests: `tools/run_tests.sh` (world, 3D picking, saving, story bot at three sizes, music render). The DS build itself is NOT verified in an emulator.

**v0.4: THE 3D PLANET IS PLAYABLE.** In 3D view the planet now sits on the touch screen and the stylus works on it directly: touch the globe to use the chosen power (paint, spawn, rain, fire, smite, gift, inspect, ...) on the exact tile under the pen, and drag to paint. Hold B and drag to spin the planet by hand; the D-pad still turns it, X / Y zoom, A changes shape, L / R change power. The pen is turned into a map tile by projecting the mesh exactly as the GPU does and picking the nearest front surface, so it works on the sphere, cube world and ring world. `gcc -O2 -DPCTEST -o pick_test pick_test.c -lm && ./pick_test` round-trips tiles through screen and back (not-grazing tiles come back within 1-4 tiles; on the ring world a tile hidden behind the near side of the ring returns what you see instead). Untested on real hardware or an emulator: no available emulator boots this build.

**v0.3: ITEMS, 3D PLANETS, LIGHT.** SELECT toggles a 3D planet view on the top screen (the touch screen keeps the HUD and map): D-pad turns the planet, X / Y zoom from far out to skimming the surface, A cycles the shape: SPHERE, CUBE WORLD, RING WORLD (the 128 x 128 map wrapped on each; it is re-painted every second). A 364-item catalogue (14 materials x 8 weapons, x 6 armours, 20 potions, seeds, foods, 40 relics, gems, god tools x 4 grades) is generated from tables; the GIFT power hands the picked item (X / Y cycle, with B held jumps ten) to a creature, a village or the land. Better things unlock as the world ages (years, folk, homes, level 3 halls); START + SELECT = free play, everything open. The 2D world now has a day and night cycle and shimmering water. PC previews: `gcc -O2 -DPCTEST -o pc3d pc3d.c -lm` draws the three planets.

**v0.2: VILLAGES.** Each people founds villages (a person with food and no home within 14 tiles settles). Villagers gather into a stockpile, eat from it, breed faster at home and stay close; villages build huts, houses, farms, towers and bigger halls (3 levels), raze to ruins if they die out, burn under fire / lightning / meteors. The orcs, goblins and ogres go to war with a neighbouring people; armies march, fight and raid. New powers: SETTLE, WAR, PEACE, BLESS; INSPECT on a building names the village.

**v0.1:** world generator (continents, oceans, ice caps, deserts, jungles, swamps, lava), 12 ground types as generated tiles, 392 creature kinds (8 peoples + 24 animal archetypes x 16 colour families, every one with its own name, shape, diet, habitat and speed), a living ecosystem (grazing, hunting, breeding, starvation, old age, dirt regrowing into grass), and 21 divine powers (paint the ground, spawn, rain, fire, lightning, meteor, smite, heal, feed, inspect).

Controls: stylus = use the power; D-pad = pan (B faster); L / R = power; X / Y = brush size, or species while SPAWN is picked; SELECT = +10 species; A = pause; START = new world. Top screen: HUD and the whole-planet map.

Build: `make` with devkitARM + libnds. PC test of the world and the ecosystem: `gcc -O2 -DPCTEST -o pc pc.c && ./pc <seed> <years>` (prints populations, writes /tmp/gg_world.ppm and /tmp/gg_zoom.ppm).

Roadmap: villages and civilisations that build, wars, disasters over time, 300+ items and tools, 300+ enemies, the story mode that unlocks free-play items, bigger planets (the world arrays are the limit: 256 x 256 fits the DS's 4 MB), saves, then PSP / Vita / 3DS ports.

## v0.9.1 menus and loading screens
The title and pause menus are now structured with sub-menus: CONTINUE, CREATE A PLANET (size, world type, difficulty, create), OPTIONS (difficulty, music volume, monsters) and EXTRAS (Aural Sculptures, Bestiary, Subspecies, Help, Credits, Debug). The pause menu has RESUME, SAVE, OPTIONS, EXTRAS, NEW WORLD, MODE, TITLE SCREEN. START leaves an Extras page back to the menu. Making, loading or enlarging a world shows a loading screen with a growing planet and progress bar.

The CPU-cache build is now the main one: `godguy_hw.nds`. The old no-cache build is `godguy_hw_nocache.nds` (build it with `GG_NOCACHE=" " ./build_hw.sh`), and `godguy_hw_safe.nds` stays as the no-sound fallback.

## v0.9.2 create a planet and ground transitions
CREATE A PLANET now has land shape (continents / supercontinent / islands), sea level, climate, wetness, amount of starting life, seed (A rolls a new one) and a live preview map; the world behind the menu is regenerated as you choose. The map now blends where biomes meet (dithered fringes, shore foam on water edges, drawn from on-demand transition tiles) and deep water and lava animate.

Volcanoes now creep: lava spreads over nearby ground (scorching life and buildings) and cools to rock in rain.

## Memory, and three platforms in step (v0.10)
- The music streams are now stored LZ-compressed in the ROM (tools/lz.py; 1.85 MB became 193 KB) and unpacked into one 99 KB buffer when a song starts. The transition-tile table became a small hash. The DS ROM went from 2.16 MB to 0.5 MB and the program now uses about 1.2 MB of the 3.0 MB of RAM it may use, so there is room for many more songs, bigger data and features.
- `./build_all.sh` builds all targets from the one shared source and runs the tests: `godguy_hw.nds` (DS, caches on), `godguy_hw_safe.nds` (DS, no sound), `godguy_dsi.nds` (DSi build, `-DGG_DSI`; normal header so a DSi runs it in DS mode), `godguy_dsi_mode.nds` (EXPERIMENTAL: unit code 3 so a DSi starts it in DSi mode with 16 MB RAM; untested, it may need proper DSi header fields) and `godguy.3dsx`. Every new feature goes into the shared source; anything only the DSi may use goes behind `#ifdef GG_DSI`.

## v0.11 kingdoms, wars and empires
Villages now belong to kingdoms (up to 8). Each has a ruler with a temper (warlike, peaceful, greedy, wise), gold, technology (nine techs), relations with every other kingdom, allies and wars. Relations drift with distance, race and temper; bad relations lead to declared wars, allies join in, soldiers march, and cities are captured (they change kingdom) instead of only razed. A beaten kingdom can become a vassal and pay tribute (and rebel later); a kingdom with five cities or two vassals becomes an empire; big cities send out colonies or revolt. Extras: KINGDOMS (list and details), JOURNAL (the last 24 events) and ACHIEVEMENTS (26). The minimap shows each kingdom's territory in its colour. Also: four new power icons, named elite/boss monsters (with a journal entry when a boss falls), a warm dawn/dusk light, GOOD VIBES and PEACE end kingdom wars too. Test: pc_kingdom.c. Worldbox's own wiki could not be read here, so the systems follow its general ideas (kingdoms, diplomacy, wars, alliances, vassals) rather than its exact numbers.

### Reworked from WorldBox's wiki (v0.11.1)
The wiki itself could not be fetched (Fandom returns HTTP 402 here), but search summaries of its pages gave concrete rules, now used: opinions are recomputed each turn from distance (touching borders -, far +), same species (+15/-10), same culture (+15/-15), alliance (+30), at war (-500), truce, territory (-1 per 5 more zones, capped -20), relative power and ruler tempers; wars come from plots (a kingdom plots for a few turns, then declares conquest war), spite wars (the WAR power) cannot be talked out of, peace comes from peace talks, kingdoms share wars with allies; each kingdom has a capital that never rebels, a maximum number of cities (3 plus one per governance tech), cities have loyalty that falls when over the limit or far from the capital, and a city may rebel (new kingdom, at war with the old one) when loyalty is negative, the kingdom is 15+ years old, the city has 10+ people and the kingdom has more than 6 cities or exceeds its limit; taking a capital shakes the loyalty of every other city.
