# BORE: GODGUY (Nintendo DS)

A god game spun off from BORE: shape a 128 x 128 world with the stylus, drop in life, and watch it live, eat, hunt and breed.

**GLOBE (v0.9).** The 3D globe is now drawn by the CPU, not the 3D hardware, so it works in every build (including the DraStic one): a lit planet with a dark side and city lights, atmosphere, drifting clouds that follow the weather, a sparkle on the water and stars; touch picking and walk mode use the same maths backwards (tested: walking faces every tile to within 1 tile). The cube world and ring world are gone; the pause menu was redrawn with a dimmed backdrop and the page counter replaced the dots.

**DS BUILDS (which one to use).** `godguy_hw.nds` (made by `build_hw.sh`) is the one that runs in DraStic: it uses NO libnds and NO calico (own ARM9 start-up, own ARM7 for sound and touch, direct hardware registers, the top screen as a tile layer) in the classic ROM layout. It has no SD saving (use DraStic's save states). `godguy.nds` (made by `build_rom.sh` / `make`) is the libnds 2 / calico build for emulators and hardware that support it (melonDS, recent flashcarts) and has saving and the globe. `godguy_hw_safe.nds` is the hardware build with all sound off, for finding crashes.

## Speed and the 3DS (step 8)
- **Kingdom answers are kept.** The cities, people and power of a kingdom, the distance between two kingdoms and a kingdom's zones are worked out once and remembered until a village or a kingdom changes (or the tick moves on), instead of walking every village each time the kingdom turn, the kingdom page or the browser asks. A redraw of the kingdom page costs about a hundredth of what it did. `-DKD_CHECK` compares every remembered answer with a fresh one (pc_speed.c does).
- **The globe is drawn only when something it shows changed**: the turn, the zoom, walking, clouds, weather, the blink of stars and city lights, a power used, or every 16 ticks. A paused or still planet costs nothing, and the picture is sent to the screen only when it was redrawn.
- **A faster shim renderer** (`shimRenderChanged` in compat/ndsshim.h): palettes as a table, map and tile lookups once per 8 pixels, and a hash of each engine's inputs so an engine that did not change is not drawn again; it says which screen changed. It gives the plain renderer's picture byte for byte (the plain one is kept as `shimRenderRef` and pc_speed.c compares them on the real program under random keys and stylus).
- **3DS**: the New 3DS speed-up is switched on (`osSetSpeedupEnable`), a screen is copied only when it changed (and for the two frames its two buffers need), the copy walks 8 x 8 blocks because the 3DS screens are stored column by column, and the sound queue is eight frames deep, filled in order. `3ds/stub/3ds.h` stands in for libctru so pc_3ds.c can test the platform file on a PC. Not run on a real 3DS: no devkitPro here.
- Crowd cost: already flat from step 3 (a bounded number of creatures walked per tick); pc_speed.c prints the cost for 1500, 6000 and 12000 creatures.

## DSi mode (step 9)
`godguy_dsi_mode.nds` (built by `build_dsi.sh`) is now a real DSi-mode build: `-DGG_DSI16` gives planets up to 768 x 768, 38,000 creatures and 128 villages in 16 MB. Its own linker script (`hw/arm9dsi.ld`) keeps the code low, puts the big arrays above the ARM7 / IPC block and ends them before a 2 MB uncached save block at `0x02C00000` (cache region 3 in `hw/crt0.s`, whole main RAM is region 1). `crt0.s` first probes the RAM (two free words 8 MB apart): on a 4 MB machine it shows a red screen and stops before anything is cleared. `tools/dsihdr.py` writes the DSi header fields (unit code 3, region flags, title id, size) and the header CRC. `godguy_dsi.nds` stays the safe DS-mode ROM with the DS sizes.
- The ARM7 now looks for the firmware touch settings at both `0x02FFFC80` (DSi mode has no 4 MB mirror) and `0x027FFC80`.
- A packed save bigger than the cartridge chip now fails ("SAVE FAILED") instead of wrapping round; `saveGameRaw` reports a failed pack.
- Tests: `pc_vilsave.c` (768 planet saves, packs, loads back identical; the packed size is printed) and `tools/dsi_emu/probe.py` (Unicorn: 16 MB gets past the probe and into `hwBoot`; 4 MB gets the red screen with the program intact).

## War counting (step 10e)
Every 256 ticks the war groups are counted again from the creatures (`wgBuild`), and each soldier asked `vilNear` for its home village, which walks every village slot: 12,000 soldiers x 64 slots in one tick (and 40,000 x 128 in DSi mode). On the PC that one tick cost about 1.1 ms against a mean tick of 0.014 ms, which on a DS is a hitch of several frames every 256 ticks. Now the villages are laid on 16 x 16 tile blocks once a count (`wvBuild`, a village goes into every block its 14-tile reach touches) and a soldier reads only the few villages of its own block (`wvNear`), in the same order and with the same test, so the answer is the same tie for tie; a block with more than 8 villages falls back to `vilNear`. The war plays out exactly as before (same state hash, same casualties); the count costs 0.3 ms instead of 1.1 ms at 64 slots and 0.4 ms instead of 2.0 ms at 128. `-DGG_SLOWHOME` counts the old way. Test: `pc_home.c`.


## Near-camera wars at big sizes (step 10g)
Up to 12,000 soldiers the war around the camera matched the full per-unit run, but at 32,000 and 60,000 the near camera under-killed by 10 to 12%: an army counted as near when its centre was anywhere in the camera window, yet its soldiers stand up to 14 tiles round that centre, so many of them were outside the window and left to the slow rotation (one visit per creature every ~2,400 ticks at 32,000), where they hardly fought. The near zone is now smaller: `FAR_MARGIN` is -2 (an army is near only when its centre is 2 tiles inside the window, so a bigger share of the fighting is settled by the far-battle roll). Against 8-seed full per-unit references (`war_ref_dsi.txt`, `war_ref_big.txt`; the old 2-seed ones were too noisy) the worst checkpoint is 4.9% off at 12,000 (was 9.6%), 4.8% at 32,000 (was 12.4%) and 6.9% at 60,000 (was 10.5%), limit 10%. -6 fixed the big wars too but made the 12,000 war over-kill near (11.0%), so -2 is the value that passes all three. Two other ideas were tried and dropped: letting near-army soldiers outside the window march, and letting far armies fight a near army by roll. `GG_BIGWAR=1 tools/run_tests.sh` runs the two big wars (a few minutes); `pc_roll` is now in the suite.

## Save and load in a rolled war (fix to step 10f)
`pc_save` showed that on the 192 planet a loaded game did not go on exactly as the game that was saved ("600 ticks later equal to the unsaved run: NO"); it began with 10f and every commit before it passes. The cause: after a save the running game still had the rolled count switched on (`wgKeep` > 0, so the averages of an army came from the slices), but a loaded game started with `wgKeep` at 0 and used the exact count (`wgBuild`) until its first 256th tick, so the two used different army averages and drifted apart. `wgBuild` now sets `wgKeep` to the creature count, since an exact count leaves every `wgOf` good. `pc_save` passes on all three planet sizes, `pc_roll`, `pc_far`, `pc_ident` and the three war benchmarks are unchanged or better (12,000 war: 4.6% off). The old "damaged header refused: NO" line was not a bug: `saveGame` keeps a `.bak` and `loadGame` falls back to it, so a damaged save still loads from the previous one. `pc_save` now checks that the loader itself refuses the damaged header, that `loadGame` then recovers from the `.bak`, that a damaged file with no backup is refused, and that a truncated file is refused; it also returns a failure status for them.

## Rulers, pan and speed (v0.13.1)
- **Ruler stats (Pack A1 of docs/GODGUY_NEXT_ADDENDUM.md).** Every ruler has STEWARDSHIP, WARFARE, DIPLOMACY and INTELLIGENCE (1 to 10), worked out from the ruler's name seed and years of rule (`kdRStat`), so nothing is saved and old saves load unchanged. Stewardship: +1 city cap per 6 points and loyalty target +2 per point over 5. Warfare: power +3% per point over 5. Diplomacy: opinion of every kingdom +1 per point over 5. Intelligence: tech cost -2% per point over 5. The kingdom card shows the four; a coronation goes to the journal. Test: `pc_rulers.c`.
- **B + stylus drag pans the 2D map** (normal and zoomed view); nothing is painted while B is held. Test: `pc_pan.c`.
- **B + A cycles the game speed** 1x / 2x / 4x.

**v0.30: VOICES AND HIDDEN TRACKS.** The music player has a vocal chop kit (`tools/gm_vox.py` builds `source/gmvox.h`, 4-bit ADPCM unpacked into a small buffer when struck): 52 chops. 24 are cut from the GBA game's voice clips and pushed hard (12 tonal chops from the sung "serenade" clip retuned to every note, 12 textures: laughter clouds, a backwards yahoo, a stuttered yeha ...); 28 are the songs' OWN voices, cut by `tools/extract_song_vox.py` from the CORPORATIONS vocal stem and the DUCK & COVER Koala project and given ten treatments (raw, cloud, backwards, stutter, low crush, squeak gate, shuffle, robot, ghost, tape stop). They play from the drum-B stream (ids 16 and up) and borrow a cymbal channel while they sound. CORPORATIONS (riff and bass brought up an octave so the DS speaker can play them, notes held to the next onset, the lead echoed) and DUCK & COVER (half-time, 88 bpm, dark mood) are in the jukebox, read from their MIDI files. 26 hidden tracks (`tools/gm4_compose.py`) retell the original BORE songs close to how they were (own key, mode, tempo and hooks; intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, last chorus, outro) with voices; they show as "?? HIDDEN TRACK ??" in the jukebox until the story opens them: chapters 1 to 14 one each, then every 4 achievements, the end of the story, 100%.

**v0.31: LYRICS ON DUCK & COVER, WAR MUSIC THAT FOLLOWS THE BATTLE.** DUCK & COVER now carries the original lyric phrases from the Koala project (`tools/duckcover/lyrics`, `lyric_events.json`), time-stretched onto the 88 bpm grid, lowpassed, wrapped in a plate-reverb tail and sounded dry on one side with a quieter stereo echo copy a sixteenth later on the other (stream-B ids `GM_VOX_LYR0` and the `GM_VOXE0` echo ids). The chop data now lives in the ARM7 binary (`hw/arm7vox.s`, 0x02390000) so the DS builds keep their RAM; decode buffers are at 0x023D0000. WAR DRUMS plays only while blows are really being struck near the camera (`warHeat`, fed by `ntHit`), not merely during a declared war, and opens up with the size of the crowd (`gmCrowd` 0..400): bass and pads alone for a skirmish, then lead, counter-line, harmony, arpeggio and glitter, cymbals and chops, then run-time timpani, crashes, tom fills and noise-and-pitch risers and falls (`gmWarExtras`, `gmSwoop`). `GMCROWD=<0..400|-1 for a ramp> ./pc_music 22 out.wav 89` renders it.

**Duck & Cover vocals, second pass.** The lyric chops are now tuned to what the song itself plays under them (`tools/duck_lyric_notes.json`, written by `gm4_compose.py`): the hook phrase is moved so its top note is the song's top note (F#4 in "from the lover that you have loved"), the rest snap to the notes the melody and bass play there; stretching is WSOLA. They sound at full level (the melody voices duck under them) (the repeat pass of that version was dropped in the third pass below).

**Duck & Cover vocals, third pass.** The first extraction had missed pads 6 and 7 of the Koala project: pad 6 is the 20 s sung verse ("to where you're loved by the gods", "hallelujah every day" ...), played once from beat 34, and pad 7 a looped syllable used for long hits and stabs. `tools/extract_lyrics2.py` cuts the verse at its silences into six phrases and adds pad 7, and every phrase now sits at the project's own bar and beat (its 145 bpm becomes 88, same length in beats): 18 lyric chops, no repeat pass. Two phrases that overlap in the project play dry (no echo copies). The packed chops grew to 309 KB; the ARM7 blob region is 0x02390000-0x023DD000 and the two decode buffers (38 KB each) sit at 0x023DD000.

**v0.32: THE 3DS SOUND (and a third Duck & Cover pass).** The 3DS build mixes the same 16 channels through a richer mixer (`SHIM_HQ` in `compat/ndsshim.h`, on a New 3DS; an old 3DS keeps the plain mixer, see `plat_isNew3DS`): cubic (Hermite) interpolation instead of nearest-neighbour (no more aliasing grit on the waves, no hiss on the drums), every melodic voice played as three slightly detuned copies spread across the stereo field (a unison chorus), volume and pan smoothed over 1.5 ms (no clicks) with an equal-power pan law, a band-limited PSG square, a Freeverb-style stereo reverb and a soft-knee limiter instead of a hard clip (denormals kept out of the reverb tails: they are very slow on an ARM11). The lyric phrases of Duck & Cover are a 16 kHz set there (`GM_HQ=1 python3.13 tools/gm_vox.py` writes `source/gmvox_hq.h`; built with `-DGG_HQVOX`; the phrases are re-cut from the Koala project at its own 48 kHz). `pc_hq.c` plays one song through that mixer (`./pc_hq out.wav <track> <seconds>`, `GMCROWD=-1` ramps the war crowd); the test suite renders three songs through it and checks for clipping.

**v0.33: QUALITY CONTROL FOR EVERY TRACK.** All 168 tracks now go through `tools/gm_up.py` (repairs, variation of repeated bars, phrase-end fills, an arc through the piece) and get a measured loudness trim (`tools/gm_loud.json`); `tools/gm_qc.py` reports before and after, see MUSIC_QC.md. The 3DS also feeds its sound for the real time elapsed and unpacks the vocal chops once at start-up.

**v0.34: CORPORATIONS' OWN VOICE, THE SAMPLED RISERS, AND THE VOCALS-CUT-OFF BUG.** (1) All 56 sung phrases of CORPORATIONS (`tools/extract_corp_lyrics.py` cuts them from the isolated vocal stem, `tools/corporations/lyrics`) now sound on the sixteenth the song sang them, at the song's own 131 bpm and pitch (the voice was 4 cents off the 12-tone grid: left alone), with a light plate tail, placed alternately left and right of centre, over the manipulated chops of the same voice and the MIDI band, which steps back 22% under each line. Measured on the 3DS mixer: each phrase correlates with the stem at 0.88 (median) and lands within 5 ms of the same offset. The 3DS has all 56 (12 kHz); the DS holds the 7 loudest (21 s, 4.5 kHz: the ARM7 blob now starts at 0x02382000, right after the ARM7 code, which gave 57 KB more room, and is full); DSi mode holds all 56 as an array in the ARM9 program (`GM_FULL=1`, `source/gmvox_full.h`). `GM_VOX_N` is 112 now, which is every id the drum-B stream has, so the Duck & Cover echo copy is the lyric's own id with the soft bit set. (2) THE BUG behind "the vocals are still not right": a cymbal channel was killed by its old id even after its sample had ended, and the id (a channel number) had meanwhile been given to ANOTHER sound, so the next hat cut the vocal phrase short (on the DS and 3DS too). `gb_hit` now kills only while the hit is still sounding (`ghEnd`). (3) The WAR DRUMS swoops are samples now (a reversed-cymbal riser with a low swell under it, and a sub-boom impact with a falling tail: `gm_kit.py` `mk_riser`, `mk_impact`) instead of PSG sweeps. (4) `pc_fix` now mixes the sound it makes so finished samples free their channels.

**v0.35: CORPORATIONS ON THE GRID, DUCK & COVER IN TUNE, AND THE 3DS PUSHED.** (1) The CORPORATIONS phrases were placed against the wrong beat zero (the timing list's first beat, 0.344 s, instead of the MIDI grid's 0.152 s from `tools/corporations/notes.json`: 190 ms early against the band) and the recording is tuned to A = 430 Hz (40 cents flat against the MIDI): both fixed. The band is now remixed on top of the MIDI (never changing its notes): a third below the lead on the harmony voice, a rising sparkle, phrase-end fills, and a riser, impact and crash into every eighth bar (`remix=True`, `gm_up.py`). (2) The Duck & Cover lyrics all share one transposition now: the Koala project is a whole tone above the new song (its hook tops out on G#4 where the song has F#4), so every phrase moves down 2.0 semitones and then onto the song's notes (before, each phrase searched for its own offset, which put "from the heavens up above" in the wrong place). (3) The 3DS mixer (`SHIM_HQ`) has 24 channels (vocals, drums, music and effects each keep their own), a VOCAL BUS (the lyrics get a ping-pong echo of a dotted eighth at the song's tempo and a bigger room than the band), a KICK SIDECHAIN (every kick ducks the melodic voices by up to 32% and lets them swell back), tape-style drive on bass voices (harmonics a small speaker can play) and a master compressor in front of the soft limiter.
