# BORE: GODGUY (Nintendo DS)

A god game spun off from BORE: shape a 128 x 128 world with the stylus, drop in life, and watch it live, eat, hunt and breed.

**GLOBE (v0.9).** The 3D globe is now drawn by the CPU, not the 3D hardware, so it works in every build (including the DraStic one): a lit planet with a dark side and city lights, atmosphere, drifting clouds that follow the weather, a sparkle on the water and stars; touch picking and walk mode use the same maths backwards (tested: walking faces every tile to within 1 tile). The cube world and ring world are gone; the pause menu was redrawn with a dimmed backdrop and the page counter replaced the dots.

**DS BUILDS (which one to use).** `godguy_hw.nds` (made by `build_hw.sh`) is the one that runs in DraStic: it uses NO libnds and NO calico (own ARM9 start-up, own ARM7 for sound and touch, direct hardware registers, the top screen as a tile layer) in the classic ROM layout. It has no SD saving (use DraStic's save states). `godguy.nds` (made by `build_rom.sh` / `make`) is the libnds 2 / calico build for emulators and hardware that support it (melonDS, recent flashcarts) and has saving and the globe. `godguy_hw_safe.nds` is the hardware build with all sound off, for finding crashes.

- **Narrative**: a cast with portraits and typed dialogue (Wisp, Mira, Gruul, Elowen, Thorgrim, the Hollow King): a scene at the start of every chapter and an epilogue before the ending and credits. The Hollow King is the last god before you, who gave up; the story is about what to do with him.

- **Weather**: rain, storms (lightning strikes the land and flashes the screen), snow; it dims the light, greens dirt, kills fire and has its own music. Rain and snow are sprites.
- **Walking the globe** (3D): Y + A turns walking on; the pad moves an avatar tile by tile, the planet turns to keep it in front of the camera, A uses the current power on it. Checked on the PC (sphere: every tile faced within one tile); the cube world is not accurate enough so walking uses the sphere.
- **Flow**: START opens a pause menu (save, volume, difficulty, monsters, mode, new world, help, title) with confirmations, so a new world is never one stray button away; tool descriptions; a picker panel showing the choice before and after for spawn / gift / mutate; page dots; toast messages.

**v0.7: SUBSPECIES, A REAL INTERFACE, THE 3DS.**
- **23 heritable subspecies traits** (venomous, thick-hided, swift, giant, tiny, fire-born, amphibious, winged, pack, regenerating, camouflaged, spitting, symbiotic...). Children inherit their parent's traits and now and then mutate; a creature can carry two. The MUTATE power (X / Y picks a trait you have met) makes a subspecies on purpose; page 5 of the top screen is the codex with live counts. These are my own design: the WorldBox wiki page could not be read from here (it sits behind a bot check), so nothing is copied from it. Monsters now also come as ELITE and BOSS ranks.
- **Interface**: the text console is gone. The top screen is a drawn interface (title screen with a spinning planet, status panels with the minimap, bars and icons, story / bestiary / codex / sculptures / debug / help windows with scroll bars), and the touch screen has a **toolbar with 10 power icons** you tap (arrows scroll; the 12 ground-painting powers show the ground they paint). The DS cannot write single bytes to VRAM, so every bitmap, tile and sprite is built in RAM and copied 16 bits at a time (the earlier build did not do this and would have drawn garbage on hardware).
- **Intro, ending and credits scenes**, a one-line narration for each chapter, tips for new players, a help page, difficulty (calm / normal / brutal), world seeds in the debug menu.
- **3DS port**: `3ds/build.sh` builds `godguy.3dsx` with devkitARM + libctru. The game runs unchanged on top of `compat/ndsshim.h`, a small stand-in for the parts of libnds it uses (tile layers, sprites, the 16 sound channels, keys, stylus). The same shim lets the real DS program run on a PC (`pc_ds.c`) with scripted input, screenshots and the sound it makes. The 3D globe is DS-only. PSP and Vita are not done: no SDK for them here.
- Tests: `tools/run_tests.sh` now also runs the real program headless.

**v0.4: THE 3D PLANET IS PLAYABLE.** In 3D view the planet now sits on the touch screen and the stylus works on it directly: touch the globe to use the chosen power (paint, spawn, rain, fire, smite, gift, inspect, ...) on the exact tile under the pen, and drag to paint. Hold B and drag to spin the planet by hand; the D-pad still turns it, X / Y zoom, A changes shape, L / R change power. The pen is turned into a map tile by projecting the mesh exactly as the GPU does and picking the nearest front surface, so it works on the sphere, cube world and ring world. `gcc -O2 -DPCTEST -o pick_test pick_test.c -lm && ./pick_test` round-trips tiles through screen and back (not-grazing tiles come back within 1-4 tiles; on the ring world a tile hidden behind the near side of the ring returns what you see instead). Untested on real hardware or an emulator: no available emulator boots this build.

### Reworked from WorldBox's wiki (v0.11.1)

### v0.14.1 speed (DS, DSi, 3DS)
Measured with a profiler on a 192 x 192 world with 2,400 creatures (a PC is roughly 50 times faster than the DS ARM9, so the figures are for comparing, not for reading as DS milliseconds):
- the creature simulation is cheap (about 15 microseconds a tick); the real costs were elsewhere:
- `kingdomTick` asked the same village-walking questions over and over inside nested kingdom loops: their answers are now remembered for the length of the tick (45 -> 16 microseconds a call, same results);
- the 3D globe was redrawn every frame (0.27 ms here, over 10 ms on a DS): it is now redrawn only when it turns, zooms, the weather changes or its 8-tick animation steps, so an idle globe costs nothing;
- the software renderer of the 3DS / PC shim: palettes are converted once a frame, tiles are looked up once per 8 pixels, and `shimRenderFast` redraws a screen only when its inputs changed (a menu, a pause or the idle status screen then cost almost nothing; the 3DS port also copies only the changed screen). World frames 0.35 -> 0.22 ms. `pc_ds.c -DRCHECK` renders every frame both ways and checks they are byte-identical (3,300 frames, 0 mismatches; the old and the new renderer also give identical pixels on all 58 tour screenshots).

### v0.16.1 one menu language, touch, rendering
- **Same structure everywhere.** The title menu now matches the pause menu: START / CONTINUE, CREATE A PLANET, OPTIONS, COLLECTIONS (browser, bestiary, subspecies, goals, jukebox), MORE (help, credits). Powers have groups: hold **B and press L / R** to jump between TOOL, LAND, LIFE, PEOPLE and DISASTER powers (the group is shown beside the power's name).
- **Touch, done carefully** (`hw/touchfilter.h`, unit-tested by `pc_touchcal.c` and `pc_hit.c`; not testable on a real DS here): the ARM7 reads the DS's own touch calibration from the firmware (two copies, newest wins; a plain linear map if they look wrong), takes 5 samples a frame, drops the extremes and averages, distrusts a frame whose samples disagree, never reports the first (bounce) frame of a press, and needs two empty frames in a row to call it a release; the ARM9 ignores sub-pixel jitter and smooths real movement a little. In the interface a tap that misses a button by up to 4 pixels takes the nearest button. 3DS: a touch within 10 pixels outside the game's area counts as the edge.
- **Rendering order.** World tiles, scroll and sprites are written right after the vertical blank begins (the thinking happens before it), the touch screen picture is copied once per blank instead of in the middle of a frame (the loading screens still show at once), and a screen identical to the one shown is not copied.
- Save files now also keep the plague, tornadoes and acid rain, so a loaded world continues exactly like the unsaved one (the save-and-continue test caught that they were missing).

### v0.17 crowds: 30,000 creatures
- The creature cap went from 2,400 to **30,000** (DS, DSi, 3DS, PC). One creature to a tile (and 16-bit tile indices) means a 256 x 256 planet cannot hold more than roughly what its land offers: a hundred thousand does not fit the model, 30,000 is about where the land runs out. A 256 world grew to ~22,000 in a 60,000-tick test.
- Cost stays flat: the creatures near the screen glide, draw and take turns at full pace (found through the tile map, not by walking the whole list); the rest take turns in rotation, a few more per tick as the crowd grows; village headcounts read the tiles around the village. The screen still shows at most 100 creature sprites (the hardware limit).
- Saves: the raw save buffer moved into normal RAM (800 KB), the packed copy gets 512 KB, and packing is bounded (a save that cannot fit fails cleanly instead of running over the ARM7). Before, a big raw save could have run into the ARM7 code at 0x02380000.
- RAM on DS: ARM9 code + data now ends at 0x022AD400 (stack 0x022F0000, heap in between).
- 3DS: fixed a missing brace in the touch edge clamp that stopped the 3DS port from building.
- Tests: the zombie bite chain is now tested deterministically (bites infect, a fallen victim rises).

### v0.18 six zoom levels
- **L + R** zooms in, **X + Y** zooms out: PLANET (the 3D globe), VERY FAR (2 pixels a tile), FAR (4), NORMAL (8, the old view and its hardware sprites), CLOSE (16) and MICRO (32).
- Very far to micro are drawn by the CPU into the same picture the globe uses, under the toolbar. The ground is the normal view's own tile art enlarged (transitions, shores, buildings, the day and night light), creatures are drawn as animated sprites (walk cycle, a hop while moving, a shadow, crowns for elites and bosses, the plague's flicker, a carried weapon), so every creature on screen shows, not only the 100 the hardware sprites allow. The pad pans the zoomed view, the stylus maps straight to tiles, and the minimap frame follows the view.
- Tile art of the visible window is cached, so scrolling builds only the new column.
- Fixed on the way: leaving the globe left stale transition tiles; the toolbar's tile numbers moved up (704) to make room for the 672-tile picture.
- Test: pc_zoom.c drives all six levels, panning and the stylus mapping (screenshots in /tmp/gg_zoom).
