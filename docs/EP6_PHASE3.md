# EP6 Phase 3: the economy and the hauling (PC only, nothing in the game calls it yet)

## Step a: source/kingsim.h, the economy
Pure C on top of `kingdom.h` (every function `static inline`, so an unused one costs no ROM). One struct, `KSim` (about 13 KB), holds the whole simulation; the realm, the building list and the map are the caller's (the ones `kgEncode` / `kgDecode` use).
- Seven building types in a table (`KS_T`): hut, house, woodcutter, quarry, sawmill, mason, storehouse. A building is a blueprint (level 0) until its cost, one good, has been carried to it. Costs, periods and sizes are numbers in the table and are not in a saved realm, so they can be tuned without breaking saves.
- Homes take one Nubbin a day (hut 4, house 8). The founding party is `KS_PARTY` (4) Nubbins with no home: without them a new realm could never build its first hut (the earlier attempt's 30-building soak sat at population 0 and "passed" because nothing checked that it ran).
- A quarter of the Nubbins (and one) stay free to carry. The rest staff gatherers first, then processors (gatherers first so wood and stone never run out of hands).
- Gatherers fell trees and quarry rock in a 14 x 14 reach, one per `period` frames. A working woodcutter plants trees too (a first version only grew a tree beside another, and the forest died out for good; the fix is in the test log). A quarry runs dry.
- Processors turn 2 of their input into 1 output. The storehouse is the way in and out of the stockpile (`R->goods`).
- THE BOOKS: `S->made[g]` is what should exist of each good (gifts and production, minus what is used up or lost at the stockpile's ceiling). `ksLedger` checks it against what does exist (stockpile + buildings + carriers). Every function that moves a good keeps it true.
- In a saved building `stock` is: low byte = goods delivered to it, high byte = goods it holds to send out. `ksInit` clamps and checks everything a damaged save could hold, and refuses overlaps, roads under buildings and more than `KS_MAXB` (40) buildings or `KS_MAXN` (768) road cells.
- Placing, demolishing (what a building holds goes to the stockpile) and roads (`ksRoadSet`, a sorted list of road cells with a binary search) live here too.
- Not run on a cart. The step has no test of its own: `tools/test_kingsim.c` comes in step c.

## Step b: source/kinglog.h, the hauling
Needs `kingsim.h`. Carriers are free Nubbins; goods move along the player's roads.
- A building that wants a good asks for a DISTANCE MAP (road steps from the building; its road-side cells are 0). Up to 12 live at once (768 bytes each). One is built at a time, 64 road cells a frame, so the worst frame stays near the average. A road change marks every map not ready. A map still waiting is not taken over for 300 frames (else requesters evict each other for ever).
- The matcher looks at 6 buildings a frame and starts at most 2 jobs. A requester (a blueprint's cost, a processor's input) gets the nearest producer that holds the good, else the nearest storehouse with it in the stockpile, whose road side is joined to the building. A producer's surplus goes to the nearest storehouse the same way (also when it wants something itself: a first version skipped this and the economy stopped).
- The carrier takes the good AT ONCE and appears at the source's road side, so nothing is promised on a shelf and the books always balance. It then takes a step every 4 frames to the neighbour with the lowest distance and hands over at distance 0.
- HEALING: a map not built yet or a cut road just makes it wait. Cut off for 180 frames: the good goes back to the stockpile (counted as an abort). Waiting for anything else for 900 frames: the same (a timeout). A road cell lost under a carrier: it steps aside, else to the nearest road, else it is sent home. A demolition sends every carrier home first, so building numbers can shift safely. `ksPrepSave` does the same before a save: a save never holds a carrier.
- `ksCheck` (books, road list, carriers, claims, map references) is what the PC test runs all the time. A route may be at most 254 road steps.
- Not run on a cart. The test is step c.

## Step c: the test, tools/test_kingsim.c (`sh tools/test_kingsim.sh`, or `sh tools/test_kingsim.sh soak 10000`)
Runs under AddressSanitizer and UBSan. Every scenario has to PROVE it ran (goods delivered, blueprints built, Nubbins moved in): that is the lesson of the first attempt, whose 30-building soak ran at population 0 and still printed ALL PASSED.
1. Economy: a realm made of blueprints builds itself in 300 days. 2. Healing: a goal that vanishes, a road that vanishes under a loaded carrier, a forced stall. 3. Save and load through the real `kgEncode` / `kgDecode` with carriers holding goods. 4. Determinism (two runs, same checksum). 5. Every cap (40 buildings, 768 road cells, stockpile ceiling, day counter, population, carriers). 6. Sixty garbage realms: refused or clamped, then run. 7. 30,000 random edits with `ksCheck` after each. 8. A soak with faults (road cut and mended, sawmill destroyed and rebuilt, woodcutter walled in and freed, stockpile filled to its ceiling, every storehouse destroyed and one rebuilt) for the 10 to 11 building spec and a 30-building stretch.
- Bugs it found while this was written: wanting producers never sent their surplus away; a first forest never grew back once cut out; wrong staffing order could starve a realm with no storehouse; a "carriers over free Nubbins" check that was wrong.
- Results (the 10,000-day run): books balanced throughout, 0 carriers stuck, about 237,000 goods delivered in the spec soak and 108,000 in the stretch.
- Frame cost, a MODEL (the cost per operation is assumed, as in `EP6_PHASE0.md`; there is no cart measurement): average 0.23 to 0.41% of a frame for the spec, 0.55 to 1.18% for the 30-building stretch; worst frame 3.6 to 8.7% (spec) and 5.0 to 12.1% (stretch), the road-cell search in the matcher being most of it.
- Memory: `KSim` is 13,064 bytes. With the map and a 40-building list: hamlet 15.3 KB, realm 22.1 KB, kingdom 29.1 KB. `RAM_AUDIT.md` and `RAM_PLAN.md` still disagree on how much EWRAM is free (the earlier figure was about 23.8 KB), so the kingdom size may not fit as it is: shrink the 12 distance maps or keep the map in ROM-backed edits before wiring it in.
- Not run on a cart or devkitARM.

## Step d: less EWRAM (`KS_MAPS` 12 -> 10, `KS_MAXN` 768 -> 512)
- `KSim` is 7,928 bytes, down from 13,064 (5.1 KB saved). With the map and a 40-building list: hamlet 10.3 KB, realm 17.1 KB, kingdom 24.1 KB (were 15.3, 22.1 and 29.1). Both numbers are `-D` options (`-DKS_MAPS=..`, `-DKS_MAXN=..`) if a build wants another trade.
- A road limit of 512 cells is plenty for the test realms (they use 50 to 160). A realm whose roads fill the limit refuses more roads, as before.
- Tried and failed in the 30-building test: 8 maps (the soak ends with the storehouse never rebuilt), 6 maps (almost nothing is delivered). Fewer maps means more waiting for a rebuild; the cost model is unchanged at 0.23 to 0.41% (spec) and about 0.46 to 0.98% (30 buildings) on average.
- A fix that came with it: a building that wants a good nobody holds no longer takes a map slot. Without it, wanters with nothing to get evicted each other's maps and a rebuilt storehouse could wait for ever.
- Bigger savings need a decision, not a patch: the map and `KSim` (24 KB for a kingdom) could live in a scratch the base game already owns while kingdom mode runs (`hhPool` 30 KB, `sprBuf`, `flPool` 8 KB or `fb` 75 KB), which frees all of it. That means the life game's data there must be rebuilt on the way back, so it is left as open question 4.

## Step e: the realm borrows hhPool (no EWRAM of its own)
`source/kingmem.h` lays the simulation, the realm, the building list and the map out inside a buffer the game hands over: `kmLayout(hhPool, SP_POOL, tier, &m)`. It is the safest way to free the EWRAM because the base game is not touched and the pattern is already in use: the creator's stage copy borrows `hhPool` through `stgClaim()` (house.h), which lets go of every baked household sprite and clears their keys so they bake again when play starts.
- A hamlet takes 10.6 KB of the 30.7 KB pool, a realm 17.5 KB, a kingdom 24.7 KB (6 KB spare). All three fit. Net EWRAM cost of the realm: 0 bytes.
- The parts are aligned (to 4 bytes on the GBA, which reads a misaligned word wrongly; to 8 on a 64 bit PC) whatever the buffer's address. A pool too small, a bad tier or a null pointer is refused (`KM_NOFIT`), never overrun.
- `tools/test_kingmem.c` (`sh tools/test_kingmem.sh`): every size, a pool starting 0 to 7 bytes off alignment, in a malloc of exactly 30,720 bytes under AddressSanitizer; then a few days of the simulation inside it (two huts built from the founding wood), `ksCheck`, `ksPrepSave` and `kgEncode`.
- THE WIRING RULES (Phase 4, written at the top of kingmem.h): enter with `stgClaim(); stgOk=0;` then `kmLayout`. While the realm runs NOTHING may bake household sprites (`hhBakeAll`, `hhSprBegin` write at the start of the pool) and the creator's stage copy must not be armed. Leave with `ksPrepSave`, `kgEncode` into the slot, and only then let the household bake again. The patch refuses to apply if `SP_POOL` is no longer 30720 or `stgClaim` has gone.
- Not compiled for the ARM and not wired into the game: nothing calls it yet. It uses `uintptr_t`, so the file that includes it needs `<stdint.h>`.

## Step f: two names renamed (found while preparing Phase 4)
`kingscreen.h` already had `ksFree()` (free slots) and `#define KS_START 10` (starting goods). The simulation's `ksFree` (release a carrier) and `KS_START` (founding party of 4) clashed with them as soon as both were in one file, and `KS_START` would have silently been 10 or 4 by include order. They are now `ksDrop` and `KS_PARTY`. A scan of every identifier the simulation defines against every other source file in the repo (kingsim.h, kinglog.h, kingmem.h against all of source/) finds no other clash, and a file including kingdom, kingsim, kinglog and kingmem together with kingscreen's own definitions compiles.
